# My racquets & strings — personal notes

Last updated: 2026-10-12

## What I like in a string

- Reference setup I love: **Solinco Confidential 16L (1.25) @ 51 lbs in a Wilson Pro Staff 97 v14**,
  for its power, spin and control.
- That suggests I like a **firm, shaped co-poly that holds tension very well and grips the ball**.
  Confidential 16 (1.30) in TWU's lab at 51 lbs:
  - stiffness 222 lb/in
  - tension loss 22.2% (2nd-best of all polys tested)
  - string-to-ball friction 0.637
  - spin potential 6.4
  - TWU has no lab data for the 1.25 gauge.
- I want a controlled, predictable, lower launch, not a launchy or trampoline-y bed.

## What I've learned about racquet specs

My measured specs (all as played: strung, with overgrip and dampener):

**Measurement basis: my numbers are "as played", with strings, overgrip and dampener all fitted.** Published specs (TW/TWU) are strung but without an overgrip or dampener; I'm assuming that, as TW doesn't state it. To compare like for like:
- Overgrip: about 5–5.5 g (Wilson Pro Overgrip about 4.9 g; Tourna Grip XL 5.5 g). It sits on the handle, so it adds roughly 0 swingweight and 0 twistweight.
- Dampener: 2–4 g (TW). TW and Tennisnerd both put it at about +2 swingweight. It sits on the centre line, so it adds about 0 twistweight.
- So **published spec + about 8 g and about +2 swingweight ≈ my as-played number**. Twistweight compares directly.

| Frame | Weight | Swingweight | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yonex VCORE (model not recorded) | 338 g | 320 | Liked it, but **wanted more plow-through** |
| Wilson Pro Staff 97 v14 | 339 g | 326 | Liked it; plow-through was enough. Wrist pain, though |
| Wilson Defyer 98 Pro, no lead | 330 g | 322 | Felt really nice on the wall; no match yet |

Bare-equivalent estimates (without overgrip and dampener), for comparing with published specs:
- Defyer: about 322 g / 320. This matches TW's 323 g / 319, so my frame is a normal one.
- Pro Staff: about 331 g / 324. TW lists 332 g, with swingweight 332 (TWU) or 325 (TW page).
- VCORE: about 330 g / 318.

- **Plow-through comes mostly from swingweight.** The VCORE and the Pro Staff weighed almost the same, but only the 326-swingweight Pro Staff felt like enough. So **my plow-through threshold is around 325+ swingweight as played**, about 323 on a published-spec basis.
- The Defyer at 322 is closer to the VCORE than to the Pro Staff, which is why the lead goes in.

## How my Defyer compares with typical player racquets

Comparison set: TWU Racquet Comparison Tool, current frames, 97–100 sq in, 315 g and over (n=187). The percentiles are my own arithmetic on TWU's listed specs, not a published statistic. My numbers are converted to bare-equivalent (as played − about 8 g and − about 2 swingweight) so they're on the same basis.

| Spec | Typical (middle 50%) | Median | My Defyer (no lead) | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strung weight | 318–326 g | 323 g | about 322 g (330 as played) | about 40th percentile: typical |
| Swingweight | 320–328 | 324 | about 320 (322 as played) | about 25th percentile: low end of typical |
| Twistweight | 14.3–15.1 | 14.6 | **13.8** | **about 7th percentile: low** |

- **Expert guidance on swingweight (strung):**
  - Tennisnerd: intermediate 310–325, advanced 320–340.
  - Roman Prokes: today's sweet spot is 320–330.
  - Tour pros are typically 340–360+.
- **What it means:**
  - Weight is normal, and swingweight is at the low end of normal for an intermediate-to-advanced player.
  - My earlier guess that extra weight sat in the handle was wrong. The overgrip and dampener explain the gap from TW's spec.
  - **Twistweight is low.** That means less stability on off-centre hits, which is bad news for a sore wrist.
- **Stock twistweight (TWU) for my other frames:**
  - Pro Staff 97 v14: **15.0**
  - VCORE 98: 13.9–14.2
  - Blade 98 v9: 14.1
  - Pure Aero 98: 15.3–15.4
  - Pure Strike 98: 15.6
- **So the Defyer as it stands is less stable on off-centre hits than my Pro Staff.**
- **Caveats:**
  - TW doesn't publish a twistweight for the Defyer.
  - Different measuring machines can disagree.

**My twistweight preference: about 14.5.** Very high twistweight (15.5+) feels less manoeuvrable to me, and my Defyer's 13.8 is a bit low. When I add lead, the target is about 2–2.5 g at each of 3 and 9, giving about 14.4–14.6 twistweight and 328–329 swingweight (TennCom's per-gram figures).

## Why I switched frames: wrist pain

- I liked the Pro Staff, but I've had **some wrist pain**, so I'm moving to a **softer, more arm-friendly setup**.
- Treat comfort as a hard constraint when picking strings, tension and lead.
- If the pain persists, see a physio. Gear changes only help so much.

## Lead plan

**Update 2026-10-19:** after playing it, I think the Defyer's problem was **instability from having no lead, not the O‑Toro Tour string**.
- **The lead goes in now:** 2–2.5 g at each of 3 and 9, targeting about 14.4–14.6 twistweight and 328–329 swingweight as played.
- **O‑Toro Tour at 51/49 is back as the Defyer string.** That's the tension originally chosen for the leaded frame.
- The "softer string" sections below are now a fallback only.

**Keep it whippier than the Pro Staff: that's the point of the Defyer.** (Revised 2026-10-19.)

- **Start with 1–1.5 g at each of 3 and 9, not 2–2.5 g.**
  - Estimate: swingweight ≈325–326.5 and twistweight ≈14.1–14.25 as played (TennCom: +3 swingweight and +0.3 twistweight per 1 g at each of 3 and 9).
  - That meets my 325+ plow-through threshold, stays at or below the Pro Staff's 326, and keeps twistweight well under its 15.0.
  - 2 g each side (≈328) would swing heavier than the Pro Staff.
- **No handle weight.** It barely changes swingweight, but adds static weight and recoil weight, making the frame more planted and less whippy.
- **Step up a further 0.5 g each side only if it's still unstable** on off-centre hits.
- **Alternative to test:** lead at 4 and 8 instead of 3 and 9.
  - By my rough physics estimate, it gives slightly more twistweight per point of swingweight, because it's closer to the throat.
  - It's unverified, so measure before and after.
- **Measure balance** at the same time as weight, swingweight and twistweight next time.

How the estimates were made:
- **Lead at 3 and 9:** TennCom's per-gram figures, +3 swingweight and +0.3 twistweight per 1 g at each of 3 and 9. Placed at about 55 cm from the butt.
- **Handle weight:** placed at about 7.5 cm from the butt. It adds about 0 swingweight and 0 twistweight.
- **Balance:** as-played balance is estimated from TW's stock strung balance (Defyer 32.39 cm, Pro Staff 32.0 cm), shifted for the overgrip and dampener.
- *Recoil weight = swingweight − mass × (balance − 10 cm)², in kg·cm². It's a rough figure because balance wasn't measured.

**Takeaways:**
- 3-and-9 lead adds swingweight and twistweight without changing recoil weight.
- Handle weight is what adds recoil (planted feel).
- Even fully weighted, the Defyer stays lighter, lower in twistweight and lower in recoil than the Pro Staff.
- **Choice: 1.5 g at each of 3 and 9, no handle weight.** It matches the Pro Staff's swingweight and keeps the Defyer's quicker, whippier feel.

## Pro Staff: Wasabi vs Mach-10 (2026-10-19)

**Pick: Toroline Wasabi 1.23, full bed, 50 mains / 49 crosses.**

The Pro Staff is low-powered but heavy (339 g, swingweight 326 as played), so the ball comes off with plenty of mass. It needs **bite and a controlled, lower flight**, not more power.

**Wasabi:**
- Square, with four sharp edges.
- TW calls it "one of the firmer and more control-oriented" Toroline strings.
- Tennisnerd: excellent spin, the ball "seems to dip in", comfortable and relatively soft for a shaped string.
- TW's playtester Troy says it's softer and more muted than O‑Toro Tour. A TW buyer says it has slightly less power than Solinco strings.
- Caveats: no lab data, and tension maintenance is only "average".

**Mach‑10:**
- Five-sided.
- Solinco markets it as "engineered for power", with a "higher power level". Tennisnerd found it lively, and too powerful in a Blackout 300.
- TWU (1.30 gauge) puts its spin in the middle of the pack: string-to-ball friction 0.486 (41st percentile), spin potential 6.0. One TW buyer said 1.15 "utterly fails" on spin compared with Hyper‑G.
- More first-hand arm-comfort reports than Wasabi, and holds tension better.
- Its main selling point is power, which this frame doesn't need.

**Tension:** 50/49 is 1 lb under my old Confidential 51. Wasabi is already softer, and the Pro Staff's mass needs some tension to keep the ball in.
- If balls drop short, go to 49/48.
- If the wrist complains, go to 49/48, or switch to Mach‑10 1.25 at 50/49.

### Pro Staff crosses with Wasabi mains

**Pick: Wasabi mains 50 / Luxilon ALU Power 1.25 crosses 49.**
- **Why ALU Power in the crosses:**
  - A shaped main with a round, smooth cross lets the mains slide and snap back. Toroline's own "Dreddy" hybrid uses that idea, pairing O‑Toro Tour with round, slick O‑Toro Snap crosses.
  - ALU Power is the softest-feeling string I have: Racketpedia 0.78, TW comfort 77.
  - It's control-oriented, with a "controlled, penetrating" flight (Tennisnerd), so it keeps the ball in.
  - Tennisnerd says hybrids extend ALU Power's short playing life.
- **Caveat:** no one has published a test of Wasabi with ALU Power. This pick is inferred from the principle above.

**Ranking of the alternatives:**
1. Wasabi / ALU Power: the pick above.
2. **Full Wasabi, 50/49:** the simplest option and the most bite. Use it as the baseline if I want to judge Wasabi on its own.
3. **Wasabi / ReString Sync:**
   - Round and slick, with good snapback, and soft and elastic.
   - But readers found it "a little too powerful", which works against keeping the heavy Pro Staff's ball in. One set broke at about 8 hours.
4. **Wasabi / natural gut crosses: no.**
   - The mains set the bed's character, so gut crosses add cost and power but not much comfort.
   - The gut wears quickly against shaped poly mains.
   - The real gut comfort setup is gut *mains*, but that adds power and launch, which is the wrong direction for this frame.

## Solinco Mach-10 in the Defyer (2026-10-19)

- **What it is:** a five-sided co-poly with "CloudFORM" softening agent. It was developed as "Confidential 4.0" / "10jx", is marketed as powerful, and comes in 1.15, 1.20, 1.25 and 1.30.
- **Plan: 1.25 or 1.20 at 48 mains / 46 crosses.**
  - TW customers who chose it for arm comfort strung 47.5–49.5 lbs; one used 51/49 in a VCORE and was pain-free.
  - Tennisnerd recommends low tension for soft polys and found it "soft on the arm", powerful, with surprisingly good snapback. It also holds tension better than Solinco's other soft strings.
  - Its extra power suits the low-powered Defyer. Tennisnerd found it too powerful in a Blackout 300 but good in low-power frames.
- **Lab conflict:** TWU's only entry, Mach 10 16 (1.30), measured **222 lb/in**. That's as firm as Confidential 1.30, and firmer than O‑Toro Tour (217).
  - Tension loss 26.3%, spin potential 6.0, string-to-ball friction 0.486.
  - Reviews say it's soft, so either the thick gauge or the test disagrees with them.
  - **Avoid the 1.30 for now.**
- **If it's still harsh, go down a gauge (1.20) or 1 lb more.** Also note one TW customer found it "harsh on elbow" at 47 lbs.

Caveats about the data:
- **Two test protocols.** The CSV (`string_performance_database.csv`) mixes two TWU test protocols. The older rows show much higher tension loss (median 46.5% vs 33.3%), so only compare tension loss within the same protocol.
  - ALU Power 125 is an older-protocol row.
- **Gauge and stretch columns are unreliable.** TWU's "Gauge Actual" and "Stretch" values change depending on how the site is queried.
- **No lab data in TWU** for ReString Sync or Slap, Toroline Caviar or Wasabi, or Confidential 16L (1.25).

## General principles I've picked up

- **16x20 vs 16x19:** a denser bed plays firmer and lower at the same tension. Drop the crosses about 2 lbs to let the mains move.
- **Swingweight:** lower swingweight means less plow and depth.
  - You can drop tension 1–2 lbs to compensate.
  - If you add mass instead, keep the tension.
- **Tension vs string choice:** tension changes launch more than the difference between similar co-polys does.
- **Toroline strings:** Tennisnerd says they're usually strung 2–3 lbs lower than a traditional stiff poly like Confidential.
- **Pre-stretching:** it reduces the later rate of tension loss (Cross & Bower, *Sports Engineering*, 2001).
  - It matters most for strings that drop tension fast.
  - It's optional for good tension-holders like O‑Toro Tour.
