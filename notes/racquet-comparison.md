# Defyer 98 Pro vs popular racquets (October 2026)

Last updated: 2026-10-12. Raw data for every row, with sources: [`racquet_specs_2026-10.csv`](racquet_specs_2026-10.csv).

## Where the numbers come from

- **Most specs** come from the Tennis Warehouse University (TWU) Racquet Comparison Tool:
  https://twu.tennis-warehouse.com/cgi-bin/compareracquets.cgi
- **Gaps** are filled from Tennis Warehouse (TW) or Tennis Warehouse Europe (TWE) product pages. Where TWU and TW disagree, both values are shown.
- **Twistweight** (kg·cm²) is published only by TWU, and TWU doesn't say whether it's measured strung. It probably is, because TWU's weight and swingweight are strung.
  - TW never publishes it, and **the Defyer isn't in TWU**, so the stock Defyer has no published twistweight.
  - "—" means no published figure exists for the current version.
- **My measured numbers** come from a different machine to TWU's. Treat differences under about 0.3 twistweight or 3 swingweight as noise.
- **Different basis.** My numbers are as played (strings, overgrip and dampener), while published specs are strung only. I'm assuming that, as TW doesn't state it.
  - Overgrip: about 5 g, roughly 0 swingweight and 0 twistweight.
  - Dampener: 2–4 g, about +2 swingweight, roughly 0 twistweight.
  - **To compare like for like, add about 8 g and about +2 swingweight to any published row.** Twistweight compares directly.
  - My bare-equivalent Defyer is about 322 g / 320, which matches TW's stock 323 g / 319.
- **Versions** are the current ones on sale as of October 2026.
- **Missing from TWU:** the 2025 Dunlop SX 300 line, CX 200 Tour 2024, Radical MP 2025, Extreme MP 2026, TF40 2nd gen and Defyer.
  - Their previous versions measured: SX 300 Tour 14.7, SX 300 14.9, Radical MP 2023 14.5, Extreme MP 2022 14.3, TF40 305 v1 14.4.

## How the Defyer compares

Typical figures below are for current 97–100 sq in frames of 315 g or more in TWU (n=187):

| Spec | Typical median | Middle half of frames |
|---|---|---|
| Strung weight | 323 g | 318–326 g |
| Swingweight | 324 | 320–328 |
| Twistweight | 14.6 | 14.3–15.1 |

**Weight: typical.**
- Almost every current 98 is sold at 323 g strung. Mine is about 322 g bare (330 as played), right at the class norm.
- Heavier: RF 01 Pro (337 g) and Pro Staff 97 v14 (332 g).

**Swingweight: low end of typical.**
- Stock Defyer: 319. Mine: about 320 bare (322 as played). That's about the 25th percentile of the dataset.
- The current 98s run from 314 (CX 200) to 331 (RF 01 Pro).
- Close to mine: EZONE 98 (320), VCORE 98 (321), SX 300 Tour (321), Pure Aero 98 (322), FX 500 Tour (322). These are 0–2 above mine on a like-for-like basis.
- Clearly heavier-swinging, i.e. more plow-through: Pure Strike 98 (330), Gravity Tour (328), Whiteout 305 (328), Speed Tour (325).

**Twistweight: on the low side.** Mine (13.8) beats only 4 of the 17 current 97–98s with a published figure.
- Similar or lower: the thin-beam control frames.
  - Prince Tour 98 (13.4)
  - Blade 98 v10 (13.5)
  - C10 EVO (13.5)
  - ATS Textreme Tour 98 (13.6)
  - CX 200 (14.0)
- About 1.5 lower than: Pure Aero 98 (15.3), Pure Strike 98 (15.6), FX 500 Tour (15.6), Gravity Tour (15.5).
- 1.2 lower than my old Pro Staff 97 v14 (15.0).
- About level with the VCORE 98 (14.2), the frame I felt lacked plow-through.

**Flex: 64, in the middle.**
- Softer: Gravity Tour (59), Percept 97 (60), Speed Tour (61), Blade 98 v10 (61), ATS Textreme (62), VCORE 98 (63), EZONE 98 (63), C10 EVO (63).
- Stiffer: Pure Aero 98 (66), Pro Staff 97 v14 (66), Pure Drive 98 (67–69), SX 300 Tour (69).

**String pattern: 16x20 is rare among the 98s.** Only the Pure Aero 98 and Pure Drive 98 share it. The Gravity MP and Shift 99 also have 16x20, but they are 99–100 sq in.

- **Pure Aero 98:** the closest match. Same 16x20 98-inch head and almost the same swingweight (≈2 higher like for like), but much more stable (+1.5 twistweight) and stiffer (66). Tennisnerd says it tends to launch higher than the Defyer.

- **Dunlop SX 300 Tour:** the stiffest (69) of these frames. A poor fit for a sore wrist.
- **Dunlop FX 500 Tour:** same swingweight as my Defyer, much higher twistweight (15.6) and moderate flex (65).

## What this means for me

**My twistweight preference: about 14.5.** Very high twistweight (15.5+) feels less manoeuvrable to me. My Defyer's 13.8 is a bit low for stability and my wrist.

- **To reach about 14.5, add about 2–2.5 g at each of 3 and 9.** Per TennCom, each gram at each of 3 and 9 adds about +0.3 twistweight and +3 swingweight.
  - That gives about 14.4–14.6 twistweight and 328–329 swingweight.
  - That clears my 325+ plow-through threshold, and stays under my Pro Staff's 15.0 twistweight.
- **If I ever switch frames, the soft 97–98s near 14.5 twistweight are the best fit:**

## Character profile: what my Defyer is built for, and who would struggle with it

This combines my measured specs, TW/TWU published specs and the reviews collected earlier. The reasoning from specs to playing style is inference, not tested.

**What sets it apart**
- **It's a 16x20 control frame, not a spin or power frame.** The only other current 16x20 98s are the Pure Aero 98 and Pure Drive 98, and both are stiffer (66–69) and more stable (15.3–15.4 twistweight).
  - TW calls the Defyer's power "Low-Medium" with a "slightly lower launch angle".
  - Rackets & Runners says it "won't blow you away with spin".
  - A TW playtester felt the crosses were spaced more like a 16x19.
- **It's quick and whippy.** Swingweight is at the low end of typical (about 320 like for like) and twistweight is low (13.8, about the 7th percentile).
  - Rackets & Runners called it "ridiculously fast", even after adding lead.
  - By the numbers, its stability is closest to thin-beam control frames like the Blade 98 v10, C10 EVO and Prince Tour 98 (13.4–13.6). Its beam, though, is a thicker 22–23.5 mm.
- **Its nearest twin on paper is the VCORE 98**: swingweight 321, twistweight 14.2, flex 63. The difference is the Defyer's denser 16x20 pattern and lower launch.
- **It's mid-flex (64) and reviewers call it comfortable.** Rackets & Runners measured 61 RA strung. It still feels "firm/connected", with a small sweet spot before customisation.
- **It's a pro-stock design.** It descends from the "Project P/Python" frame used by Khachanov, Rune and Korda, who play it heavily customised. Stock, it's a platform built to be leaded up.

**Who it suits**
- **Players who generate their own pace and spin** with fast, full swings, and want the ball kept down and predictable.
- **Players who customise.** It responds well to lead at 3 and 9, which raises stability without killing the racquet-head speed.
- **Players who like a quick racquet** at the net and on returns, and who make clean contact.

**Who would struggle**
- **Players who need free power or have compact or slower swings.** Low-medium power, a lower launch and a fairly dense bed mean balls land short or in the net.
- **Players who rely on the racquet for spin and height**, such as heavy topspin or moonball styles used to an Aero or VCORE. The bed and launch work against them.
- **Players with inconsistent contact.** Low twistweight and a small stock sweet spot punish off-centre hits; the head twists and the ball goes off line.
- **Players facing big hitters, or who block returns.** With low swingweight and twistweight it can get pushed around. TW's tester found it less stable than the Pro Staff 97 Classic.
- **Players with wrist or arm problems who hit off-centre a lot.** The twisting on mishits loads the wrist, even though the frame flex itself is moderate.

**For me specifically**
- **Good fit:** I swing fast, liked the much heavier Pro Staff, and get spin help from the string (O‑Toro Tour).
- **Watch-outs:** low twistweight with my wrist history, and stock swingweight just under my 325+ plow-through threshold.
  - The fix for both is lead at 3 and 9, about 2–2.5 g each side, giving roughly 14.4–14.6 twistweight and 328–329 swingweight.

## Defyer 98 Pro vs Babolat AeroPro Drive (the classic Nadal-era frame)

The AeroPro Drive (APD) was renamed Pure Aero in 2016. Its main versions are Cortex (about 2007), GT (2010) and 2013.
- **Sources:** TWU racquet database (strung specs plus twistweight), and TW's AeroPro Drive review page, https://www.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/racquet_reviews/apd13review.html
- **Unresolved discrepancies:**
  - TWU's older APD entries disagree with TW's review page by up to 10 g and 10 swingweight.
  - The review page's quotes refer to the "AeroPro Drive GT".

| | **My Defyer (as played)** | APD 2013 (TWU) | APD GT (TWU) | APD Cortex (TWU) | APD (TW review page) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Head / pattern | 98 / 16x20 | 100 / 16x19 | 100 / 16x19 | 100 / 16x19 | 100 / 16x19 |
| Strung weight | 330 g (≈322 bare) | 316 g | 310 g | 315 g | 320 g |
| Balance | ? (stock 6 pts HL) | 32.7 cm | 33.0 cm | 33.0 cm | 33 cm, 4 pts HL |
| Swingweight | 322 (≈320 bare) | 306 | 316 | 328 | 316 |
| Twistweight | 13.8 | 14.1 | 14.1 | 12.7 | — |
| Flex (RA) | 64 (61 measured strung by Rackets & Runners) | 69 | 70 | 67 | 69 |
| Beam | 22 / 23.5 / 22 mm | — | — | — | 23 / 26 / 24 mm |

**How they differ**
- **Power: the APD is much more powerful.**
  - It has a thicker, boxier beam (23/26/24 vs 22/23.5/22 mm), a much stiffer layup (69–70 vs 64), and a 100 head vs 98.
  - TW's testers called it "explosive from the baseline", adding "it's very stiff feeling, which is where all that extra power comes from".
  - The Defyer is rated "Low-Medium" power.
- **Spin and launch: the APD is the spin and launch frame.**
  - Its 16x19 bed in a 100 sq in head is far more open than the Defyer's 16x20 in 98. That means more string movement, a higher launch and more spin.
  - TW topspin score: 89.
  - The Defyer is designed for a lower, more controlled flight.
- **Comfort: the APD is clearly harsher.**
  - It's 5–6 RA points stiffer, and TW's comfort score was 73, its weakest category ("not the most comfortable racquet I have played with").
  - With a poly in it, it's the riskier frame for my wrist.
- **Swingweight and plow-through: similar or lower.** Both are around 316–322 depending on version, so it would feel no heavier through the ball than my Defyer.
- **Twistweight: about the same** (14.1 vs 13.8). Neither is especially stable on off-centre hits. The Cortex version was very low (12.7).
- **Control: the Defyer is clearly better.** It has a denser bed, lower launch and less power, so I can swing out harder without the ball flying.

**Summary**
- The APD gives easy power, spin and height from a stiff, open frame. It suits moderate-to-fast swingers who want help from the racquet.
- The Defyer is a control frame for players who supply their own pace and spin.
- For me, with a sore wrist and a liking for low, controlled flight, the Defyer is the better fit. The APD would give more free spin than my old VCORE with Poly Tour Pro, but it's the stiffest frame in this whole comparison.
